Open questions for step 5, from the two reviews of the evidence standard (2026-11-24). Checking how the standard applied to revision 6 turned up design gaps. They are recorded here so that step 5 answers them, and the standard in the previous comment then decides what evidence each answer needs. A draft that states each component's claims and scope, written while applying the standard, is `verification/evidence-standard-review/derivation-v2.md` on `spike-gaps`. It is input for step 5 and has not been approved. "R1" and "R2" below are the first and second review reports in `verification/evidence-standard-review/codex/`.

Needing the owner's decision:

1. Deadlines (R2, finding 1). An abort timed by a local clock leaves no journal entry, so replay cannot tell an aborted run from a cut just before the deadline, and re-abort cannot work for it (`codex/probes-2.mjs`). Proposed: the deadline is a durable sleep raced against the run, which makes the deadline a journaled cause, as R5's lost race is. A local timer may abort sooner only after the sleep's wake-up time plus a stated margin. This costs one journal entry per run with a deadline. The alternative is a local-only deadline that re-abort does not cover.
2. The outcome the executor reports (R2, finding 6). It needs a stated precedence among success, a terminal failure, a retryable failure, a cancellation and a defect, and a rule for how each failure reaches handler code: a cancellation as interruption, a child's 409 as a terminal failure of that call. Revision 5's model reports a cancelled root as `Exited Succeeded` (#34, `verification/de-novo-review-4/model-probes-out.txt:30-37`).
3. The clock sample while a cancellation is pending (R2, finding 9). Which value a turn uses if a cancellation arrives while its sample is pending.

4. Delivery and retention (R2, finding 3). Results go to the operations a live fiber awaits; runs are retained after their fiber stops awaiting them. As drafted, "race the pending operations, minus released ones" contradicts rule 1, which keeps an interrupted run's executing closure. Revision 6 should define both sets, say an issued run that has not started is dropped (A10), and say what happens to a result for an operation in neither.
5. Journal order (R1, finding 3; R2, finding 3). The claim is that results are delivered in journal order, not just in the same order live and on replay. It needs a reachability argument that the executor never builds A2's shape, where two ready results go in array order (F2), or the tie order stated. It also needs a list of which entries the order covers. F20 shows Restate's replay check accepts changed race membership and reordered awakeables, so the check does not enforce this.
6. Run records, by how the attempt ends (R1, finding 1; R2, finding 4). "Journaled while the process survives" is false: in R5's attempt 1 the process survived and the record was lost. State the cases separately: the attempt continues, a run's closure fails retryably, suspension, Restate's abort timeout, crash and kill. Scope the retryable case to failures the executor holds. Define the order between a closure settling, its result being proposed and acknowledged, and the attempt ending. Two closures failing retryably at once need a rule that waits on closures, not SDK wrappers, or the wait is circular.
7. Abort causation (R1, finding 4). R4 records a terminal failure whenever the signal is aborted (`spike/rev6/runs/abort.mjs:38`), so an unrelated retryable failure after the abort becomes terminal. Revision 6 needs a causation rule for each supported API, a policy for an unknown cause, and a statement of what a terminal abort says about the external effect.
8. Compensation calls (R1, finding 2; R2, finding 2). State five guarantees separately: admission; identity, one key per call occurrence rather than per source site; deduplication within Restate's retention; attachment; and the external effect. That last is at most once only if the target deduplicates by a stable key, since a child that crashes after acting and before journaling runs its action again (`spike/RESULTS.md:21`). A43's keys come from the driver and its attach loop stops after four ticks, so it tests neither identity nor attachment.
9. Later cancellations (R1, missing obligations; R2, follow-up). A second signal during unwinding must not interrupt handler code again. Re-attach must handle a child that has already finished, tell a failed `attach` from a child's terminal outcome, and cover a cancellation that arrives before compensation has issued anything.
10. The time rule (R1, finding 7; R2, finding 9). Restate all five parts of S8, not monotonicity alone. Overlapping sleeps need an anchor and an upper bound, so that two fully overlapping 10 ms sleeps do not add 20 ms. A link of a long sleep is credited only once its completion is delivered and the next sample applied. `max_chunk` (2147483647 ms) is separate from the SDK's own duration limit, and both need a policy for infinity and rounding.
11. Random (R2, finding 5). Draws come from one global stream: the adapter numbers fibers from its own counter (`src/adapter/State.ts:65`), and the conformance host uses one LCG. Per-fiber stability is S10's same-inputs property and rests on the scheduler being deterministic. The production host's draw source and seed are not yet specified.
12. Attempt-ending cuts (R1, missing obligations). The executor's decisions at suspension, abort timeout, crash, kill and closing, for executing runs, attached children and pending compensation. A35 and A36's harnesses were never committed.
13. Admission (R2, P3 and missing obligations). The executor checks the SDK version, protocol version and A16 settings before its first command; a refusal after commands have gone out is too late. State, per A15 entry type, what may differ on replay without a mismatch.
14. Finalizers at exit (R1 and R2, row 22). Ordinary exit and exit after a cancellation get separate statements, including whether a finalizer may issue a durable command once the outcome is decided.
15. Warnings (R1, finding 6; R2, finding 7). R6's checker accepts any warning time from 3.5 s to 7 s and needs a tighter check and a mutant. Which event the server's inactivity timer restarts from is not established. Both warnings need ordering rules at equal times and across attempts, and text that says what to change.
16. Documentation (R2, finding 10). The guarantees sheet gives the full cancellation table from comment 7120209653 and the compensation limits in item 8. The configuration advice covers the inactivity timeout, the abort timeout, and what an operator can do when the retry policy pauses an invocation. The signal lint is outside v1 (#13), so v1 cannot count it as a mitigation. The long-work example needs a fixture.

Under the standard's fixture rule, only F9 has an in-scope check today; every other fixture cited in this work needs a mutant before it discharges a claim.
