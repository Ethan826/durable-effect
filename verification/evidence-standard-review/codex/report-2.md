# Second review of the revision 6 evidence standard

Revise the draft before adopting it on #48. The new proof line correctly includes re-abort and executor-produced time. The derivation still makes several claims that its own scope rules would send back to the author. In particular, it promises replay behavior for an earlier deadline abort without saying what durable information distinguishes that history, and it attributes a per-fiber random generator to code that delegates every draw to a host.

Comment IDs refer to #48 unless indicated. “Answered” below means the second draft states the obligation correctly. It does not mean the implementation or proof obligation is closed.

## Findings that need changes

1. **Critical. Deadline re-abort loses the distinction between two histories**. Rows 23 and 25 require an immediate abort on replay of an earlier deadline abort, conditional on the journal already showing it. A locally timed abort followed by loss of the run result can leave exactly the same journal as a cut immediately before the local deadline. A deterministic replay given just that journal must make the same decision in both cases. Aborting both can skip work that never ran. Aborting neither can repeat work that already happened. R5 reconstructs an interruption from a journaled sleep winning a race. It does not establish a durable marker for an independently timed deadline. Choose a durable cause, a justified alternative external observation, or a narrower rule. State what happens if the marker's own persistence is interrupted, and include both histories and result/marker races. The bounded probe enumerates both replay decisions. `derivation.md:202–209,221–226`; `spike/rev6/rerun/rerun.mjs:55–71`; `spike/rev6/rerun/run-out.txt:18–30`; comment 7132209348.

2. **Critical. A protected compensation invocation and an exactly-once application are different claims**. A39 supplies deduplication under retained identity and A43 supplies survival of selected parent cancellations. The child can still apply an external write, lose its own result, and re-execute its action. The repository's earlier crash spike observed two callback executions and one application only with a target-side stable key. Row 8's at-most-once claim explicitly spans crashes and omits that business-level premise. Row 7 goes the other way and says each compensation completes even across its default scope of kill and crash. State separate guarantees for admission, deduplication retention, parent attachment, child outcome, and the target's applied effect. Add an adversarial child-side failure after the external action and before record persistence. Preserve the owner ruling on cancellation shielding while recording any limits that follow from A39/A40. `derivation.md:60–74,235–239`; `spike/RESULTS.md:19–22`; `spike/rev6/idem/run-out-a43.txt:10–29,31–47,58–64`; comments 7120209653, 7120729029, 7121147686, 7121240220.

3. **High. The wait-set claims directly disagree**. A run's interrupted fiber can release its await while the closure is executing. Row 2 subtracts released handles, while row 16 requires keeping this one. Comments 7121677014 and 7132209348 choose the latter behavior. Row 30 points at the distinction without specifying a rule. Put handler delivery ownership and executor retention in separate predicates. Include a retained run that settles without a receiver, a released run that never started, and another completion becoming ready while a sample or attach is pending. Also distinguish equality live and on replay from journal order. A matching pair of reversed traces meets equality alone. `derivation.md:17–32,140–146,253–257`; `spike/rev6/order/run-out.txt:114–127,195–210`; `spike/rev6/runs/run-out.txt:38–57`.

4. **High. The retry and cut claims still omit reachable distinctions**. An SDK or transport attempt failure is wider than a retryable error that the executor controls. Scope the guarantee to failures the executor can hold and say what applies at suspension, timeout, kill or crash. Row 15(d) cites A9 for crash and kill; A9/F17 is dropped-run suspension. An additional boundary is two user closures that fail retryably before either SDK wrapper publishes its failure. Waiting for every other SDK wrapper to settle creates a cycle. The fixture uses one successful sibling and one failing sibling. Make the separation between user-closure settlement and SDK publication explicit, including which failure is chosen and how the other failure is recorded. R3s's two timing columns are already emitted. Its passing shape does not establish the stronger `BeforeReject` ordering. `derivation.md:128–162,228–233`; `spike/rev6/order/run-out.txt:195–200,211–214`; `spike/rev6/runs/runs.mjs:75–103`; `spike/rev6/runs/check-runs.mjs:119–125`; `spike/rev6/runs/run-out.txt:66–93`.

5. **High. The Random row misidentifies the source, owner of sequencing, and theorem**. The adapter passes its own lifetime ID to `host.draw`. It allocates that ID from state through registration and does not use Effect's process-global numeric ID. The conformance host has one LCG state shared by all draws. Thus the adapter source proves neither independent streams per fiber nor stability for arbitrary cross-fiber interleavings. The cited `Scheduling` theorem projects away fiber IDs and compares global value prefixes. In the two-receiver example, both global value lists are `[7,8]`, but fiber A gets 7 in one trace and 8 in the other. The newer `Executor` proof states a per-fiber prefix property under the same inputs (including random inputs) and the runtime premise. Cite it and retain its conditions. Define whether revision 6 wants a global deterministic draw order, independent per-fiber streams, or the inherited same-input property. The prohibition on other randomness is a supported-program restriction with a separate guard or documentation check. `derivation.md:259–266`; `src/adapter/Services.ts:39–41,115–138`; `src/adapter/Fibers.ts:21–31`; `src/adapter/State.ts:49–69`; `conformance/host.ts:69–73,119–122`; `verification/proofs/Scheduling/Statements.v:291–298`; `verification/proofs/Executor/Statements.v:343–356`; #37 I7 and S10.

6. **High. The proof line leaves an executor-selected outcome out of the table**. A cancellation, terminal child failure, retryable failure, executor defect and ordinary success need distinct handler-visible or invocation-visible outcomes. Row 19 governs conversion for one run-abort path, row 27 leaves terminal child failure open, and row 22 covers interruption and cleanup. None states the general selection rule. In #34's older probe, a cancelled root produces `Exited Succeeded`, which is exactly the sort of selected outcome the new proof line should reach. The same problem arises if any error named “abort” is normalized into terminal or if child 409 is propagated as a new invocation cancellation. Literal pass-through payload bytes can stay outside the value limb. Wrong decoding or assignment can produce or select a different value, and the effect, journal and replay limbs still apply independently. A successful codec round trip alone can miss two implementations sharing the same wrong tag. Add an outcome row or explicit subclaims with a precedence table and cross-tag mutants. `proposal.md:44–47`; `derivation.md:164–170,191–198,235–239`; `verification/de-novo-review-4/model-probes-out.txt:30–37`; `spike/rev6/cancel/run-out.txt:303`; #34 and #37 I6/X6.

7. **Medium. Claim scopes are being used to dismiss T2 and to require proofs without a harmful executor claim**. For one aborted run in one attempt, a probe result can arrive before a warning timer, then settlement can race the rearmed timer. A single-attempt scope permits those interleavings, so row 24 needs T2 for the stated general rule. A similar case exists for row 28's grace/settlement race. Warnings alone may be diagnostic and outside T3. Conversely, row 26's observation-only clause that a hard kill can end a child with a certain status has no executor decision whose violation could cause one of T3's harms when Restate behaves as registered. Requiring T3 wholesale can turn a server premise or a documentation claim into a theorem obligation. Apply T3 to the executable cut/cleanup and result decisions, and use T1 for the external behaviors they rely on. For row 20, observing a defect message does not establish that a false stuck decision is detected before it prevents progress. For row 29, a reporting-only compatibility gate must not be treated as prevention. `proposal.md:19,44–46`; `derivation.md:174–181,211–217,228–251`; `spike/rev6/rerun/warn.mjs:40–58,78–98`.

8. **Medium. The draft confuses old negative controls, in-scope mutants and open corroboration**. F9 already swaps two winners in a saved trace and checks the same `followsJournal` predicate. I reran it and additionally mutated two adjacent winners in the main replay log. The main predicate rejected that trace. The statement that none of the existing fixtures has an in-scope mutant is false for the tested F9 claim. This does not complete the mixed-kind or subset-wait obligations. R6 still lacks an accepted exact-timing gate, and its first run never isolates all timer-reset causes. Also, #36's historical hypothesis mutant already has a corresponding gate test in this checkout. Record it as prior detection and specify what new checks revision 6 needs for its allowed-premise map. A validated finite oracle requires a correct predicate and interpretation, even when its grammar and bounds are published. `derivation.md:3–5,213 to 217,282`; `proposal.md:32–36,54,74`; `spike/rev6/order/check-sequence.mjs:18–51`; `spike/rev6/order/run-out.txt:168–175`; `verification/proofs/gate/test-check-axioms-coq.sh:101–113`; #36 comment 7073411774.

9. **Medium. The inherited time guarantee has been weakened in new claim text**. A clock that always gives 0 is monotone and satisfies row 12's claim, while returning the wrong time. Row 13 needs the actual lower and upper bounds and the anchor for overlaps. For row 14, a link whose timer fires before cancellation but whose sample is unapplied receives no credit under #37 P3/P9. “Links completed” alone permits the opposite. The SDK machine-width conversion is also different from the inherited implementation ceiling, `2147483647`. Separate those constraints, including infinity and rounding. Row 11's “same on replay” requires the common journal prefix and a pending-sample policy. The one-operation fallback addresses timing bounds. It does not waive proofs of journal correctness for a concurrent executor. `derivation.md:93–124,278`; `verification/proofs/Scheduling/Model.v:118,181–183`; `verification/proofs/Scheduling/Statements.v:195–257`; #37 X1/X2 and P3/P9; comment 7121677014.

10. **Medium. The documentation exemptions are too broad**. The owner assigns substantive behavior to an Effect default, a raw-promise signature and the signal-aware lint. A documentation statement is checked by review. A warning policy, admission policy or lint detector may decide something and still needs its own test under the project floor. Record the full cancellation table from comment 7120209653: interruption, one delivery, shielded compensation, hard kill, idempotency on crash, and durable admission at exit. Include the warning's remedial text, inactivity/abort settings, and what the operator can do when retry policy pauses the invocation. Include the unsignaled-fetch lint case for post-v1 and preserve the recommended separate service example. #13 explicitly says the lint is outside v1. `derivation.md:268–274`; comments 7120209653, 7121743796, 7132209348; #13.

## Checks and limits

I reran the round-1 R6 probe and the new `codex/probes-2.mjs` against the checkout. The new probe runs the F9 checker on the saved baseline and a replay log with adjacent winners swapped. It also enumerates both possible decisions over a fixed pair of indistinguishable deadline histories and checks the two assignments of distinct draws to two fibers. These last checks establish counterexamples within those grammars. They do not claim to model all of revision 6.

The soundness checks used wrong states to challenge the stated guarantees, including premature warnings, incompatible replay settings, and a skipped or repeated external action. The completeness checks started from obligations that should remain expressible, including existing scoped mutants and a pure claim covered by a local property test. The draft should admit both of those without turning a gate policy into a claim about what evidence can show.

I did not start Restate, recompile Coq or rerun the historical axioms-gate suite. I checked its relevant test source and #36's reported result. The register's native source line inventory and the primary-source arguments for conditional T4 entries were not independently re-derived. Several are unchanged from the first round and the second draft does not specify which theorem, if any, it adopts. No revision 6 executor or approved full grammar exists for checking the new rules end to end. The review adds a reproducer for F9's already-existing failure sensitivity. It does not close any of the newly identified executor obligations.
